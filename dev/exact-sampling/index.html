Exact Q sampling and quantum superpositions

This page develops the rejection bounds, overlap conventions and extensions for exact Q sampling. The following cat-state example applies the method to even and odd cats, with sampled Q comparisons against the classical mixture of their coherent components.

Pure overlaps and coherent sums

Superposition(states,c) forms a coherent sum, not a mixture. All components must supply normalised pure overlaps Aⱼ(z)=⟨z|ψⱼ⟩. Coherent, Fock and pure squeezed states, their products, displacements and rotations are supported. For D(β)S(ϵ)|0⟩ we use S(ϵ)=exp[(conj(ϵ)a²-ϵa†²)/2], ϵ=r exp(iθ), so the centred anomalous moment is -exp(iθ)sinh(r)cosh(r) in every representation.

Writing C=Σ|cⱼ| and Z=c†Gc, the proposal is g(z)=Σ|cⱼ| |Aⱼ(z)|²/(C πᴹ). Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[\left|\sum_j c_j A_j\right|^2 \le C\sum_j |c_j||A_j|^2.\]

Acceptance is the ratio of the left and right sides. Integrating accepted proposals gives exactly the target Q density times Z/C²; thus state.expected_acceptance predicts efficiency. Coefficients are rescaled to C=1 on construction. Common amplitude log scales cancel in rejection tests. The cached Gram matrix comes from analytic Bargmann Gaussian integration and normalised polynomial recurrences, evaluated at higher precision during preparation. This uses no Fock cutoff. Near-zero states with extremely small acceptance remain inefficient and can exhaust the configurable attempt limit. Pure-overlap capability alone does not guarantee that every pairwise Gram integral is implemented. Passive and collective-orbital states have pointwise overlaps but are not currently supported as generic superposition components. For operations wrapping an entire sum, expand the operation componentwise before nesting it in another sum. Unsupported Gram preparation raises an informative error. Finite precision can make extremely ill-conditioned Gram problems unresolved; such cases raise an error rather than inventing a normalisation.

Displacement retains the phase (β conj(z)-conj(β)z)/2 in logoverlap. Omitting it changes a subsequent superposition, even though it leaves an individual Q density unchanged.

Cats, including the small odd-cat limit

Cat(α,φ), EvenCat(α) and OddCat(α) use the two coherent components analytically, without truncation. Their direct two-Gaussian proposal has acceptance [1+exp(-2|α|²)cosφ]/2. Exact parity arithmetic avoids a spurious sin(π) phase. Even cats have acceptance at least one half. General phases close to destructive cancellation may have very low acceptance.

For an odd cat, let A=|α|², t=conj(α)z and λ=1-A/3. When 0<A<3, the alternate proposal draws |z|² from Gamma(2,1/λ) and an independent uniform phase. Its rejection probability is exp(-A|z|²/3)|sinh(t)/t|². The bound |sinh(t)/t|≤exp(|t|²/6) follows term by term from (2k+1)!≥6^k k!. The mean acceptance is λ² sinh(A)/A and tends to one as α approaches zero. The code selects the proposal with higher expected acceptance and evaluates sinh(t)/t by analytic continuation near zero. OddCat(0) raises an error. Fock(1) samples the limiting state explicitly.

Finite expansions and multimode applications

FockSuperposition(n,c) accepts sparse single-mode integer occupations or a modes × components integer matrix. It is exact for that expansion. For an approximation to an infinite state, report the supplied cutoff and omitted probability independently.

For a single mode, the coherent-state overlap and its Husimi Q density are

\[A_n(z)=\langle z|n\rangle =e^{-|z|^2/2}\frac{(z^*)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}, \qquad Q_n(z)=\frac{|A_n(z)|^2}{\pi} =\frac{e^{-|z|^2}|z|^{2n}}{\pi n!}.\]

The sampler selects occupation n with probability |cₙ|/C, then draws z=√X exp(iθ) with X~Gamma(n+1,1) (shape and scale) and an independent uniform phase on [0,2π). To apply the photon-counting rejection rule to heterodyne outcomes, substitute Aₙ(z)/√π for its branch amplitude. The common factor 1/π cancels in the acceptance ratio, whose numerator retains the coherent sum. For distinct occupations, Z=Σ|cₙ|² by orthogonality, so the mean acceptance is 1/C² for a unit-norm ket. For product Fock components, the overlaps factorise and the proposal draws each mode independently conditional on the selected component. See the references for superposition rejection.

NOON(N;phase) uses two orthogonal product Fock components and has exactly 1/2 acceptance at every N. Efficient sample generation does not imply low variance for high-order coherence estimators. The Fock-basis examples compare sampled moments with the exact coherence and apply the construction to Kerr evolution.

PhotonAddedCoherent(m,β) uses the exact identity

\[(a^\dagger)^m|\beta\rangle = D(\beta)\sum_{k=0}^m {m\choose k}(\beta^*)^{m-k}\sqrt{k!}|k\rangle.\]

There are exactly m+1 components and no coherent-state cutoff. For one added photon the acceptance is (1+|β|²)/(1+|β|)²≥1/2. SqueezedCat uses displaced pure squeezed components with validated complex overlaps. All these states inherit the exact generic Q-to-positive-P conversion; its estimator variance must still be measured for the observable of interest.

Independent validation and references

The test suite constructs annihilation matrices, displacement/squeezing matrix exponentials, parity Fock expansions and operator moments independently of the samplers. The odd-cat accepted density is also compared pointwise with a separately normalised odd Fock series. Production sampling does not use these finite matrices. Tests use modest parameters with negligible tails and explicitly check normalisation; convergence checks compare multiple cutoffs.

References for superposition rejection

  • In their 2026 preprint, Senellart, Wein and Emeriau give the |cⱼ|/C proposal weights, the weighted Cauchy–Schwarz acceptance ratio and the mean acceptance 1/C² for a unit-norm state. Their components are linear-optical branches and their outcomes are photon counts. arXiv:2611.11044v1 (2026), Sec. 6.
  • Dias and König give a general rejection sampler for measurements of coherent superpositions with weights proportional to |cⱼ|². The construction covers continuous measurements, including heterodyne detection. Senellart et al. describe their absolute-weight rule as a specialisation of this construction. arXiv:2501.17071v1 (2025), Lemma 3 and Corollary 2.
  • Reid, Opanchuk, Rosales-Zárate and Drummond give a phase-space special case in canonical positive-P. For an equal-amplitude superposition of all-vacuum and one photon in each mode, the Q coordinate is proposed from an equal mixture of the two product Fock Q laws with a rejection bound of 2. Equal coefficient magnitudes give the same weights under both rules. Phys. Rev. A 90, 012111 (2014), Sec. III.4, Eqs. (38)–(41).

References for representation conventions and state statistics

  • Olsen and Bradley (2009), with the thermal-state erratum (2016).
  • Weedbrook et al. (2012); our quadratures use [x,p]=i and vacuum covariance I/2, rather than that review's 2i.
  • Barnett et al. (2018), for factorial statistics of photon addition/subtraction and loss.
  • Meena, Kumar and Banerjee (2025), Eq. (35), for the Q loss-channel convolution.
  • Drummond and Opanchuk (2020), for broader sampling and weighted methods. Weighted ordinary W is deferred.

The small-odd proposal, binomial–Gamma positive-W law and collective-orbital construction are derived and verified here, with no claim of novelty.

Passive transformations and fixed-number condensates

Passive(input,U) uses a_out=U*a_in with a verified square unitary matrix. Supply vacuum modes explicitly, e.g. ProductState(Fock(3),Fock(0)). Each column of the Q matrix transforms by U. Internally mul! separates the mode operator from sample generation, allowing future structured-operator adapters.

For FixedNumberCondensate(N,u), u†u=1, draw a collective Fock Q amplitude c=√X exp(iθ), X~Gamma(N+1,1), and independent ξ~CN(0,I). Then z=u*c+(I-u*u†)ξ. Orthogonality and E|c|²=N+1 give E[z z†]=I+N*u*u†. No trajectory is rescaled to a fixed norm.

The specialised positive-P draws one collective Fock positive-P pair and sets α=u*a, αplus=conj(u)*ap; orthogonal modes have deterministic zero positive-P vacuum. Normal ordering makes every orthogonal-vacuum contribution vanish, proving the construction. method=:generic uses independent noise in every mode through Q conversion. Both are exact, with potentially different estimator variances; benchmark them using the same observable.

Preparation channels

Loss(state,η) implements z_out=√η*z_in+√(1-η)*ξ independently per mode, with scalar or per-mode transmissivity and E|ξ|²=1. This is the exact Q channel, including mixed-state decoherence. Shrinking a pure cat's amplitude alone would omit the environment's information about its components.

PhaseDiffused(state,σ;common=true) applies one Gaussian phase of variance σ² to all modes. common=false draws independent phases with scalar or per-mode standard deviations. In one mode the Q moment (z*)^p z^q acquires exp[-(p-q)²σ²/2]. Crescent(β,ϵ,q) retains its legacy signed q parameter, with physical standard deviation abs(q), and equals phase diffusion of the declared squeezed state. These channels sample mixed states and do not expose pure-state overlaps for coherent superpositions.

Exact pure-loss quantum channel on Q samples. Transmissivity is a scalar or one value per mode, in [0,1]. Independent vacuum environment noise is included; loss of a superposition includes decoherence and is not a pure shrunken cat.

General Gaussian states and quadrature units

Gaussian(β,V) orders quadratures (x₁,p₁,x₂,p₂,...), with x=(a+a†)/√2, p=(a-a†)/(i√2) and [x,p]=i. V is the symmetrised centred covariance. Vacuum V is I/2. The equally valid convention X=a+a†=√2x, Y=(a-a†)/i=√2p has [X,Y]=2i, vacuum covariance I, and a=(X+iY)/2. Convert an (X,Y) covariance by V=VXY/2 before passing it to this API; the complex displacement β is unchanged. Regression tests check both commutators, reconstructions of a, and vacuum sample variances.

The W covariance is V, Q covariance V+I/2, and nonnegative ordinary P covariance V-I/2. The uncertainty test is V+iΩ/2≥0, where Ω has 2×2 blocks [0 1;-1 0]. Eigenvalue tolerances scale with the input precision and covariance norm. Covariance factors are prepared once, including zero eigenvalues in classical P limits. Coherent/thermal/squeezed legacy fast paths retain their public calls; tests compare their conventions to the general covariance model.

SqueezedThermal follows the single-mode negative anomalous-moment convention. TwoModeSqueezedThermal retains the legacy SqueezedTwoMode(r,ϕ) convention ⟨a₁a₂⟩=exp(2iϕ)sinh(r)cosh(r) for vacuum input. GaussianBogoliubov uses a=U*b+conj(V)*b† and checks both canonical identities.

For PhotonAddedGaussian the state must be centred and single mode. Diagonalise its quadrature Q covariance with principal variances v₁,v₂. Expanding (x₁²+x₂²)^m gives nonnegative weights binomial(m,k) E[x₁^(2k)] E[x₂^(2(m-k))]. Each component draws independent signed squared coordinates from Gamma(k+1/2,2v₁) and Gamma(m-k+1/2,2v₂), then rotates back and divides by √2. This follows directly by multiplying each normal density by its even monomial. For m=1, choosing the inflated coordinate has probability vⱼ/(v₁+v₂), with shape 3/2 on that coordinate and 1/2 on the other. The common factor 2^-m from |z|^(2m) cancels in normalisation. Tests independently apply creation operators to finite density matrices and verify cutoff convergence. The centred formula is not applied to displaced inputs.

Direct Gaussian positive-P sampling

using QuantumPhaseSpace, Random
rng = Xoshiro(42)
state = SqueezedTwoMode(2.0, 0.0)
a, ap, b, bp = positiveP(rng, state, 100_000; method=:drummond)
ac, acp, bc, bcp = positiveP(Xoshiro(42), state, 100_000; method=:canonical)

Gaussian, Squeezed, SqueezedTwoMode and Bogoliubov support method=:drummond. Their default method=:canonical draws a Husimi-Q sample μ and independent unit circular Gaussian noise γ, returning (μ+γ, conj(μ-γ)) for each mode. The dedicated Coherent and Thermal samplers already produce conjugate pairs.

The direct sampler diagonalises the real symmetric normally ordered covariance C = V-I/2 = O*Diagonal(λ)*transpose(O) once per batch. Let L = O*Diagonal(sqrt.(complex.(λ))), so L*transpose(L) = C. Each trajectory draws independent standard real normals ξ and sets z = R̄+L*ξ, with R̄ = (√2 Re β₁, √2 Im β₁, …). It then forms the doubled pair αⱼ = (zₓ+i zₚ)/√2, αⱼ⁺ = (zₓ-i zₚ)/√2. The same complex quadratures enter both coordinates. Negative eigenvalues yield purely imaginary noise, and zero eigenvalues yield zero noise. A squeezed quadrature with negative normally ordered variance precludes a nonnegative ordinary Glauber P. The real Gaussian draws still define an unweighted positive-P law on the doubled space. Its Gaussian moment-generating function reproduces all normally ordered moments in the ensemble limit, including ⟨ab⟩ = cis(2ϕ)*sinh(r)*cosh(r) for the state above.

The total mean-square nonconjugacy Σⱼ E|αⱼ-conj(αⱼ⁺)|² equals 2 E‖Im(z)‖². For any unweighted sampler with the same second moments, the variance of the imaginary quadratures projected onto the negative eigenspace of C must at least compensate the negative eigenvalues there. The direct factor attains the lower bound 2 Σₖ max(-λₖ,0). For two-mode squeezed vacuum, this gives 1-exp(-2r) per mode, compared with 4 for canonical conversion.

At r=2 the direct sampler cut the variance of the real number-difference estimator by a factor of 5.8 and reduced its imaginary standard error from 0.258 to roundoff. The real occupation estimator variance fell by 12%.

The number-difference variance uses the trajectory estimator (n₁-n₂)^2+n₁+n₂ for its second moment, where nⱼ=αⱼ⁺*αⱼ. The linear terms restore the commutator. Subtract the squared mean difference if it is nonzero. In a two-mode squeezed vacuum the exact variance is zero, and the estimator can still have a substantial standard error at strong squeezing. The direct sampler reduces initial spreading, while estimator variance depends on the observable and stability of subsequent nonlinear positive-P evolution depends on the dynamics. The implementation uses Float64 covariance data, so extreme squeezing can lose the small quadrature variance at the scale of covariance roundoff.

Doubled positive-W sampling

Generic positiveW convolves a valid positive-P pair with independent η=ξ/√2: αW=αP+η, αWplus=αPplus+conj(η). This is an exact doubled representation. It supplies initial sampling, not a practical positive-W dynamical scheme.

For Fock states set K~Binomial(n,2/3), X|K~Gamma(K+1,3/2) and μ=√X exp(iθ), then add/subtract independent unit circular γ in the canonical pair. To verify the mixture, expand L_n(-4X/3)=Σ binomial(n,k)(4X/3)^k/k!. Transforming the old radial law by X=r² yields 2 exp(-2X/3)L_n(-4X/3)/3^(n+1). Termwise comparison with Gamma(k+1,3/2) gives weights binomial(n,k)(2/3)^k(1/3)^(n-k), summing to one. There is no finite rejection window or change of approximation at n=320. The exported Laguerre helpers and generic finite-window reject remain available for compatibility, and are not used by the default exact Fock positive-W path.

The exact doubled sampler gives Weyl-ordered moments. For a Fock state, the real part of αWplus*αW has mean n+1/2 and variance 2n+13/4. To see the variance, write its real part as X-abs2(γ). The Gamma mixture gives Var(X)=2n+9/4, and the independent unit circular noise gives Var(abs2(γ))=1.

using QuantumPhaseSpace, Random, Statistics

rng = MersenneTwister(424)
n = 3
w, wplus = positiveW(rng, Fock(n), 100_000)
R = wplus .* w

(; occupation=mean(real.(R)) - 1/2, exact_occupation=n,
   factorial=mean(real.(R.^2 .- 2R .+ 1/2)), exact_factorial=n*(n-1),
   variance=var(real.(R)), exact_variance=2n+13/4)
(occupation = 3.0157298114710698, exact_occupation = 3, factorial = 6.12821226459039, exact_factorial = 6, variance = 9.311106205691258, exact_variance = 9.25)

Use the complex product in the Weyl polynomial before taking its real part. The variance above is for the real sampled product. The ordinary Wigner central moment of |w|² is a signed integral and equals 1/4 for a Fock state.

For the canonical Q-derived construction the projection μ=(α+conj(αplus))/2 has density Q*G_(1/2)=W*G_1, where G_v(z)=exp(-|z|²/v)/(πv). It is a smoothed W, not ordinary W. Recovering W requires a separate noise-amplifying deconvolution. No projection is used to initialise ordinary-W dynamics. Weighted ordinary W, requiring explicit signed/complex weights and variance/convergence diagnostics, remains a deferred research track; arbitrary Q-derived positive-P may have unusable weight tails in such a construction.
